# Source validation — Method guidance

**Method:** `source-validation@1`
**Purpose:** Assess whether a source is suitable for a stated use.
**Required inputs:** source; intended use; validation criteria
**Expected outputs:** validation result; evidence; limitations

Apply every section below to the source before reporting a validation result — a source that
passes one check can still fail another, and the result must say which.

## Provenance

Before relying on the source, identify who produced it, under what authority or expertise, and
how you confirmed that identity, rather than accepting an unverified byline or domain name at
face value. A source whose origin cannot be confirmed cannot be validated as suitable.

## Relevance

As part of the validation, confirm the source actually addresses the intended use stated for it,
not a related but different use. State explicitly which validation criterion the source satisfies
and which, if any, it does not reach.

## Currency

For every source, check its publication or last-verified date against the intended use's
currency requirement, and flag a source that has been superseded by newer information even if it
was accurate when published. State the date basis for your currency judgment, not just a
conclusion.

## Integrity

Before treating a passage as evidence, confirm the source's content has not been altered,
selectively quoted, or taken out of its original context in a way that changes its meaning.
Compare a cited passage against its original surrounding context before treating it as evidence.
